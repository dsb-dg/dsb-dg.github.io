Overview

Can a voice agent stay faithful to a document while it speaks?

Voice agents can respond with natural, low-latency speech, but professional use requires more than fluency. An answer to a question about a clinical protocol, earnings call, lease, or regulatory filing must be supported by the source document. DSB-DG benchmarks when document-grounded voice systems retain facts, lose access to evidence, or recover grounding after context is refreshed. We evaluate factual accuracy, unsupported claims, multi-turn retention, and response latency.

Benchmark design

1,636 questions across five professional domains

We construct 1,341 grounded and 295 unanswerable question–answer pairs from 50 documents, with 10 documents per domain. Candidate questions are generated using GPT-OSS-20B with restricted document context and filtered through answerability, ambiguity, and cross-tier consistency checks. All user queries use fixed-voice 16 kHz mono synthetic speech; responses are transcribed using Whisper-large-v3 and judged for factual support by GPT-OSS-120B.

Results · Seven evaluated systems

Grounding failures are architecture-dependent

We evaluate Cascaded (Whisper → GPT-4o → TTS), Gemini-Live, GPT-Realtime, MiniCPM-o-4.5, Ultravox-v0.6-Qwen3-32B (UV-32B), SALMONN, and Moshi. The paper's main results show distinct limits of context capacity, retention, and latency.

Strong grounding is possible at 2k tokens

Cascaded leads at 90.3% GA. MiniCPM-o reaches 88.0%; GPT-Realtime reaches 87.7%; Gemini-Live reaches 87.4%.

SALMONN hits a sharp context-capacity cliff

Grounding falls from 70.3% (T3) to 37.2% (T4) — a 33.1-point drop. Its hallucination rate rises from 30.1% to 39.8%.

Good single-turn grounding need not survive dialogue

UV-32B drops from 80.0% to 75.0% between repeated early and late probes (GDS −5.0 points); Gemini-Live has GDS +3.3.

Refreshing context helps — selectively

A single refresh improves GPT-Realtime by +14.6 points; multiple refreshes improve Cascaded by +7.1, but reduce SALMONN by −4.4.

Takeaway: Reliable document-grounded voice agents require more than low response latency. Context capacity, long-turn factual retention, domain difficulty, and refresh strategy all affect whether a spoken answer is supported by the source.
